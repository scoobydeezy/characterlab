## Missing distinction
An honest statement can be objectively wrong. A deliberate lie instead requires a
speaker-held proposition, an actual choice to present contrary content and a declared
communicative purpose. Its recipient may not hear it, may not learn, or may resist it.
Truth, speaker belief, intended assertion, produced assertion and recipient belief must
therefore remain separately traceable. Recipient error alone does not prove deception.

## Conservative first profile
Compare truthful assertion and contrary assertion as two explicit candidate actions
through the existing two-option reason/dice grammar. Preserve concealment as the
qualified separate comparison; do not silently widen frozen grammars to a three-way
choice. A controlled motive for informing and a controlled motive for inducing a
contrary recipient belief can provide distinct grounds. These must be named task/goal
operands, not a Liar trait, selected-action flag or caller-supplied false sentence.

Acquire speaker belief before constructing either assertion. If it is unknown, a
contrary-to-belief assertion is unavailable; a random false Boolean is not a lie.
The operation should construct content from the frozen belief and selected action.
Do not claim general language generation or inferred knowledge of the listener.

The misdirection goal is not proof that the speaker knows what the listener currently
believes. Admit bounded listener-belief operands only through an explicit source and
consumer contract if the phenomenon actually requires them. Preserve second-order
knowledge as a separate Brief12.10 obligation.

## Required comparisons before closure
- Same speaker evidence, opposite informing/misdirection motive intervention, actual
  alternatives/reasons/choice/intent/expression preserved.
- Same intended contrary assertion with successful delivery, failed execution and
  denied receipt. Failed lies retain intent without fabricating recipient evidence.
- False speaker belief: an intended contrary assertion can accidentally match world
  truth. Deception intention must follow the speaker's knowledge, not truth labels.
- Received assertion with NoLearning and an existing contrary recipient history.
  Changed evidence availability does not guarantee successful persuasion.
- Unknown versus known false, selective recipients and full later-provenance equality
  under hidden truth/nonrecipient changes, including contested addressed draws.
- Named violating alternatives: truth-defined lying, intent-as-delivery and direct
  private-state copying. Preserve a serious alternative receiving law.
