## Concrete experiment
A character selects the same meaningful task action under a fixed context and seed.
The selected intent and contextual DecisionExpression freeze before planning/attempt.
After the attempt starts, a separate external actor either blocks it or does not.
Observer A can see the blocking act and failed outcome. Observer B sees only the
failed outcome. Later a controlled, identified report may give B additional evidence;
receipt of that report is not automatic proof that its claim is true.

The first source may use a narrow identified-interaction channel, following SOCIAL
and COMMIT precedent. It must explicitly label its recognition/source limits rather
than implying that general person/action recognition is solved. The blocker is an
actual governed event participant, not an opaque hidden cause ID copied into evidence.

## Required distinctions and expected comparisons

| Intervention | Fixed quantities | Required witness |
|---|---|---|
|External block versus no block|Chosen intent, frozen expression, prior belief, skill, probe seed|Execution differs; earlier intent/expression do not|
|Block versus incompetence|Same failed observable outcome for B|B cannot infer a hidden cause from outcome alone|
|A witnesses obstruction; B sees failure only|Same actual event|Different admitted evidence can support different causal beliefs|
|Hidden blocker/cause changes with identical safe projection|All character-admitted operands|Character outputs/state exactly equal; research truth trace may differ|
|Later report present/absent/contradictory|Original frozen history, original attempt|Only accepted evidence changes later attribution; no rewrite of prior intent|
|Repeated blockage|Prior expressions remain frozen|Later expectation may change from permitted evidence; no truth-side repair of belief|

The interpretation/update rule is not yet accepted. It must distinguish unknown cause,
observed obstruction and a fallible reported claim; it cannot use an ordinal or
research provenance as proof. Narrow explicit claims may be compared with a richer
inference model later; source convenience is not a causal-attribution theory.

## Named alternatives before allocation
- **Separated intent/evidence/outcome**: conservative baseline; admitted evidence
  governs later updates, while the frozen chosen intent remains unchanged.
- **Success-as-intent**: replaces or qualifies intention from success/failure alone;
  should fail the obstruction/incompetence and frozen-history comparisons.
- **Omniscient cause**: imports the actual blocker into B's belief; must fail identical
  safe observation under different hidden causes. Adversarial control, not admission.
- **Outcome-only learning**: discards witnessed causal information; may match B but
  should miss A's positively witnessed distinction.
- **Last admitted claim versus accumulated evidence**: serious learning alternatives;
  select an exact bounded law only after evidence schemas and conflicting-report
  semantics are specified. Neither is declared universally necessary in advance.

## Scope boundary: obstruction is not coercion
A freely chosen action blocked after selection can still express the actor's intent.
An externally imposed action is not automatically a freely authored choice. This
first slice must not subtract identity evidence merely because execution failed,
nor declare the Brief's coerced-action case qualified. Pre-choice coercion needs its
own admitted context and qualification contract after the obstruction seam exists.
No chosen third-party communicative goal or language generator is claimed by the
first controlled report source. Those remain in the SOCIAL/communication package.
