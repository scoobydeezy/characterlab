## Result
Concentrated successful practice can leave a character dependent on one known
satisfier. Learning another effective satisfier permits actual substitution when the
first becomes unavailable. The bounded task uses one shared relief goal, not a separate
motive for each option or an authored dependence trait. Demand is an externally
admitted opportunity-local instruction, not a physiological Need or chronic deficit.

Both primary histories perform four successful, observed practices. Concentrated
history practices A four times; diversified history practices A/B/A/B. At the same
current demand and availability, A is removed and B is available for three free probes.
Concentrated history has unknown B relief and no acquired B response: no chosen intent,
no attempted delivery and three unserved opportunities. Diversified history knows B
relief: it actually chooses B, attempts it, delivers one unit and learns the admitted
result at each probe. Both start the probe with four total physical training successes;
final physical totals are4 versus7, respectively. This isolates training distribution
rather than unequal prior total reward or current demand.

This qualifies concentration of the learned repertoire and successful alternative
use. It does not establish compulsion, exclusive affective attachment, withdrawal,
clinical dependence, healthy functioning generally or a general resource law.
