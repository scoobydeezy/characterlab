## Selected first direction

Use three separately governed conditional estimates in a successor profile:

1. adverse outcome in the current threat-cue context;
2. adverse outcome during a visibly bounded baseline trial without mitigation;
3. adverse outcome during the matching visibly bounded mitigation trial.

The first supplies current likelihood. The latter two supply an explicit, fallible
contrast for perceived mitigation; their support must be separately attributable.
The existing two-key BELIEF models and their frozen images remain unchanged. Reuse
their learned-state authority and arithmetic only under an accepted successor.

Control must not be a renamed available-action bit or an unqualified success label.
The baseline and mitigation conditions must come from observed context and observed
action/inaction, not event scheduling, caller trial index, hidden intervention flags
or a dispatch parent. Missing context or incomplete observation must be capable of
withholding a learning update even when the physical intervention is unchanged.

## Candidate inference, not an accepted causal law

For a known available mitigation and learned baseline risk b>0 with treated risk t,
compare relative reduction max(0,(b-t)/b) against absolute reduction max(0,b-t).
The former is the proposed first normalized mitigation coordinate. It preserves a
distinction between current threat likelihood and learned mitigation effectiveness.
The latter remains a serious comparator; do not casually treat its units as identical.
Neither is an identified causal effect in uncontrolled observational data.

If b is unknown or zero, mitigation efficacy is unidentifiable in this profile;
return Unavailable rather than perfect safety, zero efficacy or a pseudocount.
Known t>=b gives known zero reduction, distinct from unknown. Perceived availability
is an independent operand. A complete known-empty mitigation set can imply no
available control; an incompletely observed set cannot. Keep per-action estimate,
availability and aggregate control as distinct trace quantities.

Three estimates are needed because using the same scalar for current likelihood
and the baseline of a mitigation test would constrain the supposedly independent
likelihood/control intervention. This is a source-domain extension, not a new
psychological primitive or an earned general causal-learning theory.
